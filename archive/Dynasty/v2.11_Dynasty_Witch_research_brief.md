## 1. Age curves by position

RBs fall off after 28, receivers hold until 30 to 32, and tight ends and quarterbacks last longest. RB value falls years before production does.

| Position | Peak ages | Decline starts | Proposed "For sale" age |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RB | 23 to 26 | After 28 | 26 and older |
| WR | 25 to 28 | 30 to 32 | 29 and older |
| TE | 25 to 32 | After 32 | 30 and older |
| QB | 25 to 36 | 37 | 34 and older |
| Running QB | 25 and under | 32 to 33 | 31 and older |

The last column is my proposal, not a finding. It asks whether a player is still inside his prime in 2028.

**RBs (data)**

- 57.4% of top-12 seasons since 2000 came at ages 23 to 26, and only 16.3% of top-24 seasons came at 29 or older. Sample: 52 RBs with 8 or more seasons. [Elequin, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-lifecycle-of-a-dynasty-running-back-fantasy-football/)
- The top-12 rate drops from 19.8% at age 28 to 11.8% at 29. Sample: 1,011 seasons over 13 years. [Tagliere, 2020](https://www.fantasypros.com/2020/07/at-what-age-does-a-running-back-decline-fantasy-football-2020/amp/)
- The average peak season is at 25.5, and 24.8 since 2016. Sample: 343 seasons of 15 or more PPR points a game. [Braude, 2026](https://apexfantasyleagues.com/peak-age-nfl-running-back/)
- Value added over a replacement RB peaks at 23 and nearly vanishes by 25. Sample: 210 RBs drafted in NFL rounds 1 to 3. [Kates, 2020](https://apexfantasyleagues.com/why-youre-not-selling-running-backs-early-enough-in-dynasty/)

**Wide receivers (data)**

- 48.4% of top-12 seasons came at ages 25 to 28, and 84% of receivers peaked between 24 and 29. Sample: 73 receivers with 8 or more seasons. [Elequin, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-lifecycle-of-a-dynasty-wide-receiver-fantasy-football/)
- 38% of declines hit between 30 and 32. A decline is a 30% drop in points per game, or a final season. [Elequin, 2025](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/dynasty/dynasty-lifecycles-players-with-age-cliff-concerns-for-2025)
- The top-12 rate stays between 12% and 20% from 24 through 32, then falls to zero at 33. Sample: 1,000 seasons. [Tagliere, 2019](https://www.fantasypros.com/2019/07/fantasy-football-at-what-age-does-a-wide-receiver-decline-2019/amp/)
- At 28 a receiver has about 50% of his career production left, against 20% for an RB. Sample: about 150 receivers since 1990. [Parsons, 2013](https://www.footballguys.com/article/parsonsthenewreality7)

**Tight ends and quarterbacks (data, thinner)**

- Tight ends: the top-12 rate runs 27% to 31% at ages 25 to 27 and is still 35.7% at 30. Sample: 455 seasons. [Tagliere, 2017](https://www.fantasypros.com/2017/04/fantasy-football-at-what-age-does-a-tight-end-succeeddecline/amp/)
- Quarterbacks: steady from 25 to 36, with the sharpest drop after a 14th season. The study is from 2012 and covers 1970 to 2011. [PFF, 2012](https://www.pff.com/news/age-of-decline-qb)

**ESPN's 2023 aging study (added in v2.8)**

- RBs: among those who debuted since 2000, points per game fell 25.2% from age 28 to 29, and total points fell 37.0%. In the since-2010 group the drops were 16.8% from 26 to 27, 8.4% from 27 to 28 and 15.3% from 28 to 29, on only 11 RBs.
- RB touches: players 28 or older took 13.8% of all RB touches in 2022, down from 16.4% in 2012.
- QBs: rushing production fell 25.7% from age 26 to 27, then held within 0.23 points a game from 27 to 29. The best three-year span was 23 to 25. The study stops at 29.
- Receivers: the biggest jumps came in year two (43.0% in points per game) and year five (24.5%). Year five was the peak, at 17.5 points a game.
- Tight ends: second-year tight ends nearly doubled their rookie output (98.5%), held it through year seven, and didn't decline much until about 30.

Source: Cockcroft, ESPN, July 3, 2023, from the PDF you supplied. Sample: the top 100 players at each position to debut since 1970, and the top 25 to debut since 2000 and since 2010. That is an elite-only sample, so the caution below about players who lasted applies to it too.

What it changes: nothing in rule 1. It supports 26 for RBs, since the recent decline starts at 26 to 27. It supports 30 for tight ends. On QBs it shows rushing dropping at 27 and then holding, which Allen has beaten so far: his rushing points rose from age 26 to 29. It says nothing about ages 30 and up, where the window question sits.

**Running QBs (added in v2.3)**

This is my own count from NFL season stats for 1999 to 2025, in your league's scoring. A running QB is one who averaged 4 or more rushing points a game through age 29, across at least two seasons of 200 or more pass attempts. Nineteen qualify.

- **Same QB, one season to the next:** running QBs lost 1.9 total points a game per year at 30 to 31 (8 cases) and 2.1 at 32 to 33 (5 cases). Pocket QBs lost 0.5 at 30 to 31 (49 cases) and gained 0.2 at 32 to 33 (37 cases).

**Leone's takeaways**

- Early seconds "carry a little more risk than late firsts but roughly the same amount of upside," and may be undervalued.
- Late seconds "are much worse bets than early seconds and not that much different than third-round bets."
- Draft position inside rookie round 3 "is mostly irrelevant."
- Rookie round 1 offers "more elite upside for RBs, more viable safety for WRs."
- Rookie round 2 RBs "really trounced" rookie round 2 receivers. He reads that as the market undervaluing the second tier of rookie RBs.
- Rookie round 3 flips: "WR is the smarter bet as you get deeper into drafts."
- The rare rookie round 1 tight end is much safer than later ones, yet none of the nine had an elite season. Rookie round 3 tight ends had the best elite rate.

**Zachariason on draft capital: tiers, not a ranking (added in v2.10)**

A second episode tests how well NFL draft position predicts a player's best two seasons out of his first three, for the 2011 to 2023 classes. Every result is in the appendix.

- RBs taken in the top 16 almost always hit. From pick 17 to pick 50 there is no relationship between draft slot and production (25 RBs).

- After pick 100, receivers mostly produce nothing. RBs keep a small edge for NFL round 4 over NFL round 7.
- His conclusion: "draft capital works more in tiers than it does as a precise ranking system."

## 4. Market timing

Picks are cheapest during the season and peak when they're on the clock. Productive veterans sell best in-season. You are selling veterans for picks at the right time on both counts.

**Picks (data)**

- KTC prices for 2024 and 2025 picks rose through the season, the playoffs and draft season, then fell from June to August. The author's rule: buy in September, sell in May. Sample: one calendar year, 2023. [Stein, Footballguys, 2024](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-dynasty-investor-rookie-pick-valuation)
- A pick two years out trades at a discount: 8% for a mid first, 19% for a mid second, 25% for a mid third. Source: KTC values, 2022 against 2024 picks. [Liberman, Dynasty Nerds, 2022](https://dynastynerds.com/?p=115081)

**Picks (opinion)**

- Picks "peak in value when they're actually on the clock" and "reach their nadir during the season." The rest of the piece is paywalled. [Harstad, Footballguys, 2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-dynasty-in-theory-week-04)
- "There isn't a worse time to target picks than right before the NFL Draft." [Footballguys roundtable, 2021](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2021-dynasty-best-time-deal)

**Veterans (opinion only)**

- The same roundtable says to sell productive veterans during the season, when they're scoring and teams need help. It also calls older receivers "wildly affordable" in the months before the draft, which is the seller's worst window.
- I found no study that measures veteran prices in-season against the offseason. This rests on analyst agreement.

**Veterans (data on yearly decline, added in v2.9)**

- Among players with a top-100 startup price, 53% of rookies rose the next year, typically by 5 spots. Only 30% of veterans rose, and the typical veteran fell 16 spots. The rookie group is 79 players from the 2014 to 2019 classes.
- In the top 200, 48% of rookies rose (97 of 203) against 41% of veterans. The typical rookie fell 5 spots and the typical veteran 9.
- At prices 50 to 100, 56% of rookies rose (50 players) against 31% of veterans.
- 6% of top-100 veterans had no price at all a year later.
- Each rookie class pushed about 13 new players into the top 100.

Source: Zachariason, the same episode as in section 2.

This is the first measured support for selling veterans in a rebuild. It shows their price falling from one offseason to the next. It doesn't say whether in season or the offseason is the better time to sell, so that part of rule 6 stays opinion.

**What it means for you**

- Sell veterans before the Week 14 deadline. Waiting for the offseason means selling into their cheapest window.
- Taking picks as payment now is buying at the low point. Hold them and they gain as the draft approaches.
- A 2028 first costs you little against a 2027 first, about 8%. Picks in later rookie rounds two years out lose a fifth to a quarter, so avoid being paid in those.
- If you'd prefer proven players to rookies, the time to trade a pick is while it's on the clock.
- No source pins down a best week to sell. Until one does, the trigger is a contender's need, such as an injury to a starter.

## 7. Rebuild roster shape

I found no study of what a rebuilding roster should look like. Everything published is opinion, so the proposals here are built from the data in sections 1, 2 and 5.

**What analysts say (opinion)**

- Build around young receivers, add a long-window quarterback, be patient with tight ends, and leave RBs for later. [Pintado, Dynasty Nerds, July 2026](https://www.dynastynerds.com/?p=169228)
- The same piece suggests selling receivers at 27 to 28, tight ends around 28 and quarterbacks at 32. Those are earlier than the production data in section 1 supports.
- Rebuilders should sell veterans before the deadline and wait until after the NFL draft to buy veteran RBs. [FantasyPros staff, 2025](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/03/dynasty-strategy-advice-how-to-build-your-roster/)

**What the data supports**

- **Receivers first, RBs last.** A receiver at 28 has about half his production ahead of him and an RB about a fifth. RBs also produce as rookies, so they can be added the year you plan to compete.
- **The top 4 is where consolidation pays.** On Leone's rates, two early seconds give about the same odds as one mid first: a 36% chance of a viable player and 22% of an elite one, against 38% and 23%. Three early seconds still fall short of one top-4 pick, at 49% and 31% against 70% and 50%.
- **Late seconds look overpriced. Early seconds don't.** KTC prices a late 2027 second at 87% of an early one, 3,948 against 4,547 on Oct 2. Leone has it at 75% of the viable rate and 29% of the elite rate, and finds late seconds little different from thirds.

**Proposed rules**

1. **No position quotas.** The only fixed numbers are the healthy bodies a legal lineup needs: 2 QB, 4 RB, 5 WR and 2 TE. Every spot past that is judged by the stash tests in section 5.
2. **Consolidate upward, toward top-4 picks.** When a trade offers one better asset for two lesser ones, she leans toward the one. For picks, late seconds and thirds are the pieces to package. Early seconds are kept unless they help reach a top-4 pick.
3. **The Invisible Player rule carries over, weakened.** The roster spot a 2-for-1 opens is worth only the best available stash, which in a league rostering about 350 players (353 on Oct 3) is usually a low-odds one. She still names him.
4. **Picks beat extra players on a full roster,** since a pick needs no spot.

**What it means for your roster**

- Your 31 players are 2 quarterbacks, 7 RBs, 17 receivers and 5 tight ends.
- The receiver group is where consolidation applies: 6 are veterans aged 29 or older and 7 are stashes from NFL round 4 or later, or undrafted.
- You own two 2027 seconds. Roster 12's projects as an early second, since that team is 0-6, so it is one to keep. Yours is an early second only if you miss the playoffs. If it lands late, it is the one to package.

**His conclusions**

| Point | In his words or mine |
| --- | --- |
| Tiers | "Draft capital works more in tiers than it does as a precise ranking system." |
| Small gaps | A gap of 15 to 20 picks inside a tier has explained little. NFL round boundaries are arbitrary, which is why his model uses the raw pick |
| Top RBs | Draft RBs from the top half of NFL round 1 almost blindly. The position flattens after the top 16 to 20 |
| Later RBs | After that, look deeper than draft capital |
| Receivers | Receivers from NFL rounds 2 and 3 behave like one talent pool. Prefer NFL round 2 in general, without forcing it |
| Your own evaluation | If you like the player picked 54th more than the one picked 34th, take him. The data backs that |

**His own cautions**

- The pick ranges are his choice. "You can pick and choose your end points."
- R squared and binning answer different questions. R squared asks how smoothly production falls from one pick to the next. Binning compares whole groups, and can hide how different the players inside a group are.
